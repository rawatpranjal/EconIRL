## Paper-To-Package Translation

Hotz and Miller (1993) is the inversion source. The package implementation
keeps the core idea: recover continuation objects from a policy and transition
law, then estimate payoffs from a pseudo-likelihood rather than solving a
Bellman fixed point at every parameter guess.

Aguirregabiria and Mira (2002) enters through the NPL loop. The current
validation uses the NPL path, not only a one-step Hotz-Miller estimate. The
result JSON records both requested and realized policy iterations.

The package validation differs from the papers in important ways:

- the canonical validation cell is synthetic and supplies true transitions;
- the first-stage CCP has complete state-action support in the committed run;
- value, policy, Q, reward, and counterfactual truth are generated by the DGP;
- support diagnostics are part of the claim, not optional housekeeping.

Magnac and Thesmar style identification warnings matter here even when they
are not a public-page focus. Discount factor, reward scale, reward location,
and state/action support must be fixed or diagnosed before treating a CCP run
as structural evidence.
